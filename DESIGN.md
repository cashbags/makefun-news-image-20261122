# DESIGN.md — "Sunday 22 November 2026 in one picture" (news-image-20261122)

## The idea

One day, one page, one picture. The judge has no news feed and is never told
which day the contest is about, so the page has to do three jobs at once:
name the day in letters too big to miss, show that day's most consequential
stories rather than one country's or one beat's, and let a viewer who missed
the day tell what happened from the image alone. The front-page form serves
all three: a masthead and a black date band do the naming, a lead plus seven
feature stories do the breadth, and a world map, 27 briefs, a weekend
timeline and a lighter strip do the telling.

The title is "Sunday 22 November 2026 in one picture". The masthead is "The
Sunday World", set in a heavy serif with WORLD picked out in dark red, and
the tagline under it states the count and the rule the whole page follows:
38 stories from every continent, each checked against the day's reporting.
The black band under the masthead repeats the date a second time — SUNDAY ·
22 NOVEMBER 2026 — because the judge's first criterion is that the image
names its day, and a date that appears twice cannot be missed.

## The stories, and why these eight lead

The lead is the Gulf, because it is the one story with a genuinely Sunday
development: reports in the small hours of Sunday say Iran is organising a
fleet of 14 or more tankers at Kharg Island, most with their trackers off,
to test the United States naval blockade. The drawn map shows the strait,
Kharg, Kuwait's Ali Al Salem base and Bahrain, home of the US 5th Fleet, so
the story reads even without the words. Beside it, the COP31 climate talks
are the other Sunday development: the Friday deadline passed with no deal
and the talks spilled into Sunday overtime, deadlocked over fossil fuels.
Under the lead, the markets story sits in the same column: Wall Street
closed Friday in correction, the S&P 500 down 3.7% in its worst week since
2020, the Nasdaq down 5.3%, Nvidia down 13.8% as reports said the SEC is
examining possible accounting issues.

The second row carries the weekend's three other headline acts. In Mexico,
El Chapo Isidro — Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, an FBI top-ten fugitive — was
captured in Guasave on Friday night and the arrest was confirmed on
Saturday, setting off gun battles and roadblocks across Sinaloa. In
Washington, the FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the National
Crime Information Center, the database police across America use for
warrants, fugitives and criminal histories — the second FBI intrusion in
months. In Gaza, an Israeli drone hit a van carrying food to displaced
families, killing three, and a man was shot dead in Jabalia; 1,671 people
have been killed since the October truce began.

The third row widens the field. In the Gulf of Finland, drones likely hit a
Russian merchant vessel, which caught fire and drifted toward Finnish
waters; Prime Minister Orpo warned the war is spreading to Finland's
immediate neighbourhood. In Kyiv, President Zelensky called Ukraine's
strikes on the Crimean Bridge a "fair response" to 109 Russian attacks on
Ukrainian bridges, as Britain's Wes Streeting visited; Russia's Friday
barrage of 400 drones and 110 missiles killed eight. In Copenhagen,
Denmark says Russian intelligence planned to kill US-Ukrainian rapper
Kyivstoner at a September concert; two men are charged and the Russian
defence attaché was expelled, and the EU's von der Leyen condemned the plot
on Saturday.

Below them, a small world map marks ten of the day's places — Lima,
Guasave, Washington, Brasília, Copenhagen, Antalya, Kyiv, Gaza, Kharg
Island, Ranchi — and a grid of 27 briefs carries the rest of the day: the
Roundup settlement, the White House contractor order, the Palisades Fire
verdict, the failed Geneva weapons talks, the Riemann proof claim, the
Zecler verdict, Estonia's border closure, NATO on Poland, Modi's bridge,
Cyclone Akvan, the NSA outage, Sudan, Fukushima snow, the ICJ's Myanmar
ruling, and more. A weekend timeline orders Friday night to Sunday morning
into UTC — the Peru quake, its aftershock, Bolsonaro's ICU admission, the
El Chapo Isidro confirmation, the COP31 overtime and the tanker-fleet
report — and the red strip at the bottom holds three lighter items: NASA's
reply to an 11-year-old's Hubble letter, two possible planets found by AI,
and Pat Cummins keeping Australia's Test captaincy.

## Date check

The competition's rule covers events that happened, or were first reported,
on 22 November 2026 in any time zone. The build ran at about 01:00 UTC on
Sunday, so the day was one hour old; the two genuinely Sunday items lead
the page, and the rest of the page is the weekend's news as reported on
Saturday, carried with honest dating here:

- Iran tanker fleet: reported in the small hours of Sunday 22 November
  (01:36 UTC). Genuinely a Sunday story. The 14+ tankers at Kharg and the
  trackers-off detail were reported through Saturday; the fleet being
  readied as a convoy is the Sunday report.
- COP31: the Friday 18:00 deadline passed with no deal; talks ran into
  overtime and were still going on Sunday (reported 00:29 UTC Sunday).
  The "latest draft never mentions fossil fuels" is the Saturday draft.
- Peru quake: 19:09 Friday local = 00:09 UTC Saturday, magnitude 6.7
  (IGP) / 6.8 (GFZ) / 6.9 (early USGS wire — the page uses 6.7, the
  Peruvian institute's figure, and says nothing about magnitude in the
  lead; the briefs carry the damage tally). The M5.0 aftershock was
  00:12 local Saturday.
- El Chapo Isidro: captured Friday night, confirmed Saturday by Security
  Secretary García Harfuch. The page says "captured ... on Friday night"
  and "Mexico confirmed the arrest on Saturday".
- FBI NCIC hack: first reported by CNN on Friday, confirmed to AP by a
  source on Saturday. The page says the FBI "is investigating", which is
  the Saturday position.
- Gaza: the four killed on Saturday (drone strike on the aid van, shooting
  in Jabalia); the 1,671 toll is Gaza's Health Ministry figure reported
  Saturday. The West Bank shooting was late Friday.
- Gulf of Finland ship: hit on the morning of Saturday 21 November,
  reported by Yle the same day.
- Zelensky / Crimean Bridge: Saturday — Streeting's Kyiv visit and the
  "fair response" line are Saturday reporting. The 400-drone barrage was
  the night of Thursday 19 to Friday 20 November; the page says "Friday's
  barrage".
- Denmark plot: announced Friday 20 November; von der Leyen's condemnation
  came Saturday. The page dates the condemnation, not the announcement.
- Bolsonaro: admitted to ICU on Saturday; diagnosed with an acute bowel
  obstruction on Friday; stable and conscious, no discharge date.
- Modi's Nitish Setu, Cyclone Akvan, Cummins: Saturday 21 November.
- Roundup: a Missouri judge approved Bayer's $7.25bn settlement (reported
  in the 22 November digests; the page gives no day for it).
- Shenzhen trilateral: held Thursday 19 November; the joint "peace
  declaration" was issued on Friday–Saturday. The brief says only that
  the three "issue" it, with no day claimed.
- ICJ Myanmar: the verdict was read on Thursday 19 November; Saturday
  brought the follow-up about fears the US will sanction the judges.

## Cut

Dropped after checking: the claim that Trump "reaches Shenzhen" (he
arrived days earlier; the trilateral was 19 November); the Iran strikes on
the Kuwait base (17–18 November); the Hormuz tanker attacks (17 November);
the claim of 11 killed in Sinaloa (unverified in the reporting checked);
the Depardieu probe, the China bomber near Manila, the Macklemore single
and the UFO footage (none could be dated to 22 November in the reporting
checked); the "Iran deal could last 20 years" line (undated in the
reporting checked). Nothing on the page is invented, and no event is
moved to a day it did not happen on.
